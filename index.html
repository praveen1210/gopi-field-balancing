What is field balancing?

Every rotor has some unbalance — uneven mass caused by manufacturing, wear, dust build-up, corrosion or repairs. When it spins, that uneven mass creates a force that shakes the whole machine.

Field balancing (in-situ balancing) corrects this on site, with the rotor installed in its own bearings and running at its real operating speed. We measure vibration with precision sensors and a phase reference, add a trial weight, calculate the exact correction, and fix permanent weights until vibration is within the acceptable limit.

Signs you need balancing

  • High vibration at 1× running speed
  • Frequent bearing failures
  • Loud noise, humming or rattling
  • Cracked foundations, loose bolts
  • Vibration after a rotor repair or cleaning
  • Excess power consumption

Benefits

  • Longer bearing & seal life
  • Minimal downtime — no dismantling
  • Lower energy cost
  • Safer, quieter operation
  • Balanced in real operating conditions
  • Documented results for audits

How a balancing job works

  1. 1

    Book online

    Fill the order form with machine details. We call back to confirm date and scope.

  2. 2

    Initial survey

    On site, we measure vibration at each bearing and confirm unbalance is the main cause.

  3. 3

    Trial weight run

    A known trial weight is fixed and the machine is run again to measure its effect.

  4. 4

    Correction

    The instrument calculates the exact weight and angle. Permanent weights are welded, bolted or removed.

  5. 5

    Verify & trim

    Final run to confirm vibration is within limits. Trim balancing if needed.

  6. 6

    Report

    You receive a report with before/after readings and recommendations.

What we need from you on site: safe access to the machine, a stop/start permit (lock-out/tag-out), ability to run at normal speed, and welding or drilling support if weights must be fixed permanently.

Standards we follow

We balance to ISO 21940-11 (formerly ISO 1940-1) balance quality grades and judge machine condition using ISO 10816 / ISO 20816 vibration severity limits.

The target grade depends on your machine type. Common grades:

GradeTypical machines
G 16Agricultural machinery, crushers, drive shafts
G 6.3Fans, pumps, flywheels, general industrial rotors, motors
G 2.5Gas & steam turbines, turbo-compressors, machine-tool drives, precision motors
G 1Grinding machine drives, small high-speed armatures

Lower G number = tighter tolerance. We'll recommend the right grade for your machine.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to remove the rotor from the machine?

No. Field balancing is done with the rotor in its own bearings at site. That's the main advantage — minimal downtime.

How long does a balancing job take?

Typically 2–4 hours per machine, depending on access, start/stop time and how many correction runs are needed.

What if the vibration is not due to unbalance?

Our survey checks the vibration spectrum first. If the cause is misalignment, looseness, bearing damage or resonance, we'll tell you and recommend the fix instead of balancing blindly.

How are correction weights fixed?

By welding, bolting, clamping or by removing material (grinding/drilling), depending on the rotor. We'll agree on the method with your maintenance team.

How is the job charged?

Usually per machine, plus travel for out-station sites. Emergency and multi-machine jobs are quoted separately. Contact us for a quote.

Do you provide a report?

Yes. Every job includes a report with before/after readings, correction weight and angle, and the balance grade achieved.
